# Research: teeth and sleep milestones (sourced)

Researched 2026-09-27. Every figure below was read in the opened source (PDF text extracted, or abstract fetched via Europe PMC because PubMed served a captcha). Nothing is extrapolated except where marked **(derived)**, which means simple arithmetic on quoted numbers.

## How to read the numbers

- **JSPD 2019 (Japan)**: 日本小児歯科学会, 小児歯科学雑誌 57(1):45-53, 2019. National survey of **8,724** healthy Japanese children aged 3–47 months, 29 dental schools, fieldwork 2015–2016. Per-tooth **mean ± SD in months** by sex (Tables 3 and 4), plus the **youngest and oldest age at which that tooth was seen erupting** (最小−最大月齢). The society's official "standard eruption period" (Table 8, 標準萌出期間) is **mean ± 1 SD, rounded**: 「表 8 の各歯種の平均値±1 S.D. の正常範囲を表示する」. So Table 8 covers roughly the middle two thirds of children (about 68% if the data are normal, which the paper says they are: 「乳歯の萌出時期についても正規分布しており」). **About 1 child in 6 is earlier and 1 in 6 is later than the band, and that is normal.** The min–max column is what was actually seen in healthy children.
  - Caveat: the "人数" column is small (≈50–100 per tooth) because only children seen *mid-eruption* (<1 mm through the gum) count toward the timing; the 8,724 total feeds the prevalence curves.
  - URL: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jspd/57/1/57_45/_pdf
- **ADA (USA)**: "Primary Tooth Development" chart, ©2012 ADA. **Ranges only**, with no stated statistical meaning (no n, no SD, no percentile). Treat as a clinical rule of thumb. URL: https://www.mouthhealthy.org/-/media/project/ada-organization/ada/mouthhealthy/files/activity-sheets/adaprimarytoothdev_eng.pdf
- **UFSBD (France)**: the fiche gives no per-tooth ranges, only 「Les premières dents de lait font leur éruption six mois après la naissance」 and 「A 30 mois on a ... 20 dents」. Too vague to use for windows. It is only good for the French wording. URL: https://www.ufsbd.fr/wp-content/uploads/2013/08/Fiche-Dents-laits.pdf

**Column convention in the tables below**: early and late = the JSPD mean ± 1 SD band (Table 8), taking the union of boys and girls. Typical = the JSPD mean, averaged over the two sexes **(derived)**. The observed min–max and the ADA range go in the notes.

### Context lines for teeth (usable as UI copy, all sourced)

- **Born with a tooth**: 「The incidence of natal teeth is approximately 1:2,000 to 1:3,000 live births. The most commonly affected teeth are the lower primary central incisors.」 (Leung & Robson 2006, *Natal teeth: a review*, PMID 16708508). Adekoya-Sofowora 2008 (PMID 18408782) independently gives 「about one in every 2,000 to 3,000 live births」. Confidence: High. URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16708508/
- **No tooth yet at 12 months**: I found **no sourced prevalence** for this, so do not print a "1 in X". What is sourced: JSPD saw the first tooth (lower A) erupting in healthy children as late as **14 months** in boys and 13 months in girls (Table 3 「3-14」, Table 4 「3-13」), and the ±1 SD band ends at 9 months, so about 1 in 6 children is later than 9 months **(derived from normality)**. Safe copy: "Certains bébés en bonne santé n'ont leur première dent qu'après 1 an."
- **Teeth at 1 year (Japan)**: 「日本人小児は前回調査と同様，今回調査でも 1 歳時は平均 8 歯であり」 (JSPD, read at the 50% eruption rate). Japanese children have more teeth at 1 year than US White or Black children (mean 4.4 and 4.5 teeth at 1 year, Warren, as cited by JSPD). For a baby in Japan, JSPD is the right reference, not ADA.
- **Secular trend**: JSPD 2019 is about 1 month earlier than the 1988 Japanese norms (lower A 6.8 vs 8.0 months in boys, 7.5 vs 9.0 in girls). Many Japanese clinic websites still show the old table.
- **Sequence** (JSPD, girls): 「A→A→B→B→D→D→C→C→E→E」, which is lower central, upper central, upper lateral, lower lateral, first molars, canines, second molars. Several neighbouring pairs are not significantly different, so the order often varies.

### Context lines for sleep (for the variability message)

- **Pennestri et al. 2018**, *Pediatrics* 142(6):e20174330, n=388 (Canadian MAVAN cohort), longest uninterrupted stretch by maternal report:
  - Abstract: 「we found that 27.9% to 57.0% of 6- and 12-month-old infants did not sleep through the night」, and 「no significant associations between sleeping through the night and concurrent or later mental development, psychomotor development, or maternal mood」. Europe PMC abstract of PMID 30420470.
  - Exact cells, quoted in the review Tham et al. (PMC9925493): 「At 6 months … 37.6% of infants slept for less than six consecutive hours at night, while 43% of infants slept for eight consecutive hours」 and 「At 12 months … 72.4% of infants slept six consecutive hours at night and 56.6% slept eight consecutive hours」. So **43% of 12-month-olds do not sleep 8 hours straight**, which is normal.
  - McGill press release: 「At six months old, a slightly higher percentage of girls than boys slept for eight hours straight. (48 % vs. 39 %)」. https://www.mcgill.ca/newsroom/channels/news/parents-shouldnt-worry-if-their-infant-doesnt-sleep-through-night-6-12-months-age-291524
  - **Relevant to a nursing app**: 「sleeping through the night was associated with a much lower rate of breastfeeding (P < .0001)」. Frame this as an association, not as "breastfeeding causes waking".
- **Why Henderson and Pennestri seem to disagree** (50% at 3 months vs 43% sleeping 8 hours at 6 months): Henderson measures the **first time ever**, Pennestri the **usual longest stretch**. The app's "tap once" model matches Henderson. The copy should say that one good night does not mean every night is good from then on.
- **Henderson 2010 also supports the existing ban on the "sleep regression" idea**: 「The largest mean increase (504 minutes) in self-regulated sleep length occurred from 1 to 4 months」.
- **Galland et al. 2012**, *Sleep Med Rev* (PMID 21784676), 34 studies: infant total sleep 「12.8 (9.7-15.9)」 h (mean, ±1.96 SD); night wakings 「0 to 3.4 wakes per night for infants (0-2 months), to 0-2.5 per night (1-2 year-olds)」; 「predominantly Asian countries had significantly shorter sleep (1h less …)」. These are reference values, not one-off milestones, so they give no timeline item. They are useful as reassurance copy. I could not open the full text, so there is no per-age nap count from Galland.
- **Iglowstein 2003**, *Pediatrics* (PMID 12563055): 「The most prominent decline in napping habits occurred between 1.5 years of age (96.4% of all children) and 4 years of age (35.4%)」, so almost everyone still naps at 18 months.

## Rejected

| candidate | reason |
|---|---|
| Sleep "regressions" (4, 8, 18 months, etc.) | Banned by project policy. Henderson 2010 shows 1–4 months is the fastest-improving window. The word "régression" is never used. |
| Growth spurts, Wonder Weeks | Banned by project policy. |
| "Fait ses nuits" as a single age norm (e.g. "by 6 months") | No consensus definition (5h, 6h, 8h, 22–6). Pennestri shows 28–57% don't at 6–12 months. Use the explicit timed items above or nothing. |
| twoNaps (window) | Weissbluth gives only 「well established by 9-12 months」. No onset or spread was sourced. Fabricating early/late would break the evidence rule. Keep it as text at most. |
| Drops all naps | Outside 0–24 months (96.4% still nap at 18 months, Iglowstein). |
| "Stops night feeds" / weaning night feeds | No sourced age norm found. It is a parenting choice, and in a nursing app it would read as a target. |
| Teething symptoms (fever, diarrhoea) as milestones | Not asked for and not a milestone. Also a known myth area; leave it out. |
| "No tooth at 12 months: 1 in X" | No prevalence found. Only the qualitative line (observed up to 13–14 months in JSPD) is sourced. |
| UFSBD per-tooth ranges | The UFSBD fiche has none. Numbers found on French blogs or brand sites (elmex, calmosine) are secondary and were not used. |
| Part 3: first hair, first haircut, first shoes, other "firsts" | No age-norm data found or expected. First shoes follow walking, which is already a motor milestone. Skipped. |
