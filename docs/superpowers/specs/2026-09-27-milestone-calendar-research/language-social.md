# Language, social and cognitive milestones, 0–24 months: sourced age windows

Research date: 2026-09-27. Every figure below comes from a document I opened and quoted. When a cell says "—", I found no sourced number for it and left it blank rather than guess.

## How to read the columns

- **early**: the earliest *sourced* age at which the skill is typical. It is either the start of a published typical range (Dosman 2012; low-quality evidence, no percentile), a 25th percentile (Wordbank), or a "commence à" age from the carnet. It is **not** the 1st percentile or a biological minimum.
- **typical**: a 50th percentile or median where one exists (Wordbank AoA = age at which 50% of children produce the item; Wordbank "combines"; Schneider 2015). Otherwise "—".
- **late**: the age by which *most* children do it. It is almost always the **CDC/AAP 2022 age, which is the ≥75th percentile**. It is not a 90th/97th percentile. A 90–99% corroboration is given in the notes when one exists (Van Wiechen, SWYC, Wordbank).
- Ages are in months. Van Wiechen ages are published in weeks. I converted them (weeks ÷ 4.35) and kept the original weeks in the quote.

**Evidence strength warning, from the sources themselves.** Dosman 2012 rates every communication, cognitive and social-emotional age in its chart as "Low- or moderate-quality evidence … No percentiles provided". Only its gross-motor and self-care ages rest on high-quality percentile data. For the CDC 2022 language items, a 2023 JSLHR paper (Journal of Speech, Language, and Hearing Research) found a "striking paucity of evidence". I only saw that sentence in a search-result summary, because the abstract page returned 403 to me, so treat it as unverified. Language milestones are therefore much softer than the WHO motor windows the app already uses. The UI should not show them with the same precision.

## Caveats to carry into the UI

1. **"late" ≠ "worry" age.** CDC ages are 75th percentiles, so 1 child in 4 is still expected not to show the skill at that age. The app should not colour a gap past "late" as an alarm.
2. **Sources conflict by 3–6 months** on several social/cognitive items (hidden toy, body parts, responds to name, lifts arms). That is the real state of the evidence. Showing windows as ranges is honest, but single-month precision would be false precision.
3. **Parent-report bias**: SYF15 notes some parents report words "implausibly early (e.g., 4 months)". The app's "Elle le fait !" is the same kind of data.
4. Premature birth: DOS12 says correction for gestational age is needed below 37 weeks. That applies equally here.
